Check Your iPhone Trip Tracker: Missing & Partial Drives

To check a trip tracker, compare its history with a separate record of drives you know you took. Count missing trips, inspect route completeness and note unwanted recordings separately. One overall percentage can hide very different problems.

Written by Drivio. This is a review method you can use with your own drives, not a published benchmark or a promise of a particular capture rate.

Check setup before measuring

For Drivio, complete the automatic tracking setup and review one familiar drive. Note the iPhone model, iOS version, app version, location and motion permissions, and whether Low Power Mode was enabled. Keep these conditions in your log so you can compare like with like.

Keep a separate two-week checklist

After parking, write down each drive independently of the app. Do not build your reference list from the tracker’s history: that would leave out the very drives you are trying to detect as missing.

  • Expected drive: date, approximate departure and arrival, and a short route label.
  • Recorded: yes, no or partial. Check that both the beginning and end are present.
  • Route: note gaps, split recordings or an unexpected route shape.
  • Context: short errand, usual commute, passenger ride, permission change or phone restart.
  • Next check: what you changed and what happened on the next comparable drive.

A recorded trip is not automatically a complete trip. Keep a partial route in its own category rather than counting it as fully captured.

Download the blank trip review worksheet (CSV). Open it in Numbers, Excel or another spreadsheet app. Keep route labels general if you plan to share the file.

Calculate two different measures

Illustrative example, not Drivio test results: you independently logged 20 drives. The app recorded 18 of them, but two recordings missed part of the route. That gives a recorded-drive rate of 18/20 = 90% and a complete-drive rate of 16/20 = 80%. These describe your sample; they do not predict performance on every phone or route.

Count unwanted recordings separately. A passenger journey may be a real vehicle trip while still being irrelevant to your personal driving log. Review whether it belongs in the record you are building.

When a drive is missing or partial

  1. Check current permissions against the setup guide.
  2. Record the approximate time and what was missing before changing settings.
  3. Repeat a comparable route when convenient and review the result after parking.
  4. If the problem persists, contact Drivio support with the app and iOS versions and a description of the failure. Share only the route information needed to investigate.

Mark missing drives as gaps in your review. A note about a missing drive is not a recovered GPS route. Do not silently treat partial mileage as a complete measurement.

Keep reliability and battery tests separate

Navigation, screen use, charging and trip duration can all complicate a battery comparison. Use the battery review guide and record conditions rather than assuming a change in battery usage proves a change in tracking accuracy.

If your goal is comparing commute patterns, use only comparable complete records in your weekly commute review. Keep your excluded drives visible so the comparison remains understandable.
