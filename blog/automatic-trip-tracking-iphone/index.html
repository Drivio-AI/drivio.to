How to Track Mileage on iPhone Automatically

To track driving mileage automatically on iPhone, set up a trip-recording app, allow background location and motion access, then review a short test drive. In Drivio, recorded drives include a route, distance, and start and end times. Here is the setup and the review process.

Set up automatic mileage tracking in Drivio

  1. Install and open Drivio. It requires iOS 18 or later. Complete the first-run setup before expecting background recording to work.
  2. Enable background location. In iOS Settings, find Drivio’s location permission and select Always. While Using the App does not provide the same background access. Keep Precise Location enabled for route recording.
  3. Allow Motion & Fitness. This lets Drivio use activity signals alongside location when deciding whether a drive is starting.
  4. Take a short, familiar drive. After parking, open the Trips tab and check that the route and distance look reasonable. Review the start and finish rather than assuming every segment was captured.

You do not need to start a timer for each drive after setup. Detection can still be affected by signal conditions and iOS behavior. For permission help, use our trip recording support guide.

What a mileage record tells you

A recorded drive helps answer where you went, when you left, and how far you drove. Several drives can become a Journey: for example, the drive to a trailhead, lunch in town, and the return home.

For a recurring route, keep a daily commute log and compare similar days. For travel memories, review suggested photos and add notes after the drive.

If a drive is missing or incomplete

Nothing appears after a drive
Check Always Location and Motion & Fitness access, reopen the app, and review its permission status before another test.
The route starts late or has a gap
Check whether you started in a garage or passed through weak-signal areas. A partial route is not a complete mileage record; review it before using its distance.
A passenger ride is recorded
A phone can detect automotive motion without proving that you are the driver. Review recorded trips and remove entries you do not want to keep.
A stop splits a day into multiple drives
Keep the individual records for review, or group related drives into a Journey when you want to tell the story of the whole outing.

Does automatic tracking use battery?

Yes. Active route recording uses location services. Drive duration, reception, screen use, and Live Share affect consumption. Compare similar days in Settings → Battery; there is no single daily percentage that applies to every phone. See our battery-use guide.

Personal trip history or business mileage reports?

Drivio focuses on personal drive history and road trip Journeys. If you need business classification, reimbursement workflows, or specific export formats, verify those requirements before choosing a tracker. Our MileIQ and Stride comparison explains the different product focuses.

How background recording works

Drivio combines location and motion signals; vehicle connection signals can also help detection. No individual signal guarantees that a particular drive will be recorded. Apple describes background access in its Core Location documentation.
