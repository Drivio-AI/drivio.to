Choose a Private Mileage Tracker: A Practical Checklist

To evaluate a mileage tracker’s privacy, check what happens to a drive from recording to deletion. An App Store label, a subscription price, or the words “local-first” cannot answer every question on their own.

In this guide
  • Five checks before using a tracker
  • Use the same worksheet for each app
  • Try the controls with a non-sensitive sample

Five checks before using a tracker

  1. Permissions: read why background location and motion access are requested. Check which permissions are optional and whether you can use the core product without them.
  2. Default storage: find out whether trip records remain on the phone, upload to the developer, or use a cloud account you control.
  3. Optional features: repeat that check for backup, sync, live sharing, and exported files. A local default does not mean an enabled cloud feature stays local.
  4. Sharing access: distinguish public links from invited accounts. Find the session lifetime and the control that actually stops sharing.
  5. Deletion: distinguish deleting a trip, ending a live session, deleting a cloud record, and closing an account. Ask what copies can remain.

Record “not documented” when you cannot find an answer. Do not turn a missing explanation into a confident claim that the company sells data. Likewise, a paid subscription does not prove that no data leaves the device.

Try the controls with a non-sensitive sample

Review settings before using a private home or work route as a test. If you test sharing, use a trusted recipient and check the difference between stopping updates and deleting the session. Do not uninstall an app just to test deletion: you could lose local history while leaving cloud copies untouched.

How Drivio fits this checklist

  • Local history: recorded drives are stored on the iPhone by default, without a Drivio account.
  • Backup: the optional backup feature uses your iCloud Drive.
  • Live Share: location and session records use Apple’s CloudKit. Public links and private invitations have different access rules.
  • Ending a share: private sessions can continue across drives. Stop a session to stop updates; delete it to remove its CloudKit records. Recipients may retain information they already saved.

Read privacy labels alongside the policy

Use the App Store privacy-label guide as background, then inspect the features you plan to use. If your priority is work-mileage reporting, compare the required workflow too; privacy and reporting capabilities are separate decisions.
